The Order of Creation.

← Atlas↓ Sources

In the era of the Fatimid caliphate (909–1171), the Ismāʿīlī Shīʿī caliphate that rivaled the Abbasids from North Africa and then Cairo, Ismāʿīlī thinkers built a Neoplatonic account of how the world comes from God. The version drawn here follows Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī's Paradise of Submission, written in the thirteenth century while he worked among the Nizārī Ismāʿīlīs of Alamut, as presented in lecture, with details from the week's readings. Read it from top to bottom: each level comes from the one above it.

How to read the diagram.

The Originator (God) is outside of the chain because, otherwise, emanation would imply He shares His being with the world. The dashed lines indicate creation ex nihilo (ibdāʿ), and the "=" means the Command and the First Intellect are one being, which takes God's usual place as first cause and bearer of the divine names.

Other versions

Ismāʿīlī cosmology was never one fixed system. Nāṣir Khusraw, who often follows al-Sijistānī, has one Intellect and one Soul: the Word and the Intellect are "two only in speech," and the Soul makes the spheres, the seven planets and the elements from prime matter as its own body (Nāṣir Khusraw, 27, 38–40, 85; Walker, 78). Ḥamīd al-Dīn al-Kirmānī (d. after 1020) replaces the Intellect–Soul pair with ten intellects on al-Fārābī's model (Walker, 84–87). Al-Ṭūsī, writing after Avicenna, adopts Avicenna's ten intellects and the Intellect's threefold self-knowledge while keeping the Originator above the whole chain (Lecture 9.2).
